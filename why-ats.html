What is an ATS?

A smart filing cabinet for CVs

When you apply online, your CV usually lands in an ATS first. It stores and organises every application, then recruiters search through it, a bit like Google.

1

You apply

You send your CV through the company's website or a job portal.

2

The ATS reads it

It pulls out the text: your jobs, skills, school and contact details, and files them.

3

Recruiters search

They search for skills and job titles from the job post, and look at the best matches first.

4

A person skims

A recruiter takes a quick first look, often just a few seconds, to decide who to call.

ATS-friendly, explained

What the robot reads easily, and what trips it up

Fancy designs can look nice to people, but software reads text, top to bottom. Tobi tried both.

Easy to read

  • ✓One simple column, top to bottom
  • ✓Clear headings: Experience, Education, Skills
  • ✓The same skill words used in the job post
  • ✓Common fonts and real, selectable text
  • ✓Dates and job titles written plainly

Hard to read

  • ✕Two or three columns side by side
  • ✕Tables, text boxes and icons
  • ✕Skill bars and charts (“Excel: ●●●○○”)
  • ✕Text hidden inside images or a photo-only CV
  • ✕Creative headings like “My Journey”
Myth buster

Tobi busts a famous myth

Hold up! Let's check the facts before we panic.
MYTH
75% of CVs are automatically rejected by robots.
FACT

There's no solid study behind that famous number. In a 2025 survey of 25 recruiters by Enhancv, 92% said their ATS doesn't auto-reject CVs for formatting or missing keywords. People still make the decisions.

SO WHAT'S THE REAL PROBLEM?

Recruiters search the ATS for skills and skim fast. If your CV uses different words or a messy layout, you're not rejected by a robot. You're just never found, or skipped in seconds.

Source: Enhancv recruiter survey (2025), via IT Brief. Small sample of US recruiters.
